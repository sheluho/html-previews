Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my home needs new attic insulation?

Common signs that you need to upgrade your attic insulation include unusually high energy bills, drafts in the upper floors, uneven temperatures between rooms, and the rapid melting of snow on your roof during winter (which can lead to damaging ice dams). A professional assessment can determine the current R-value of your insulation and identify areas of critical heat loss.

What is the advantage of spray foam over traditional fiberglass?

Spray foam insulation provides a superior air seal compared to traditional fiberglass batts. As it is applied, it expands to fill gaps, cracks, and hard-to-reach cavities, creating a dense barrier that blocks both heat transfer and air infiltration. This comprehensive seal not only maximizes energy efficiency but also helps keep out moisture, dust, and pests.

How long will new vinyl siding last on my house?

Modern, high-quality vinyl siding is engineered for exceptional durability and can easily last 20 to 40 years, depending on the specific product line and local weather conditions. It is resistant to rot, pests, and peeling, meaning you will never have to paint your house exterior again. Routine cleaning with a hose and mild soap is typically all that is required to maintain its appearance.

Is fiber cement siding worth the higher initial investment?

Yes, for many homeowners, fiber cement siding is highly worthwhile. It offers the authentic look of natural wood without the susceptibility to rot, termites, or fire. While the upfront cost is higher than standard vinyl siding, fiber cement is incredibly robust, holds paint beautifully for years, and can significantly increase the resale value and architectural integrity of your property.

Can replacing my house siding improve my home's energy efficiency?

Absolutely. While siding itself primarily protects against weather, the siding replacement process provides the perfect opportunity to add rigid foam insulation beneath the new exterior layer. This "wrap" reduces thermal bridging through the wall studs, working in tandem with your interior home insulation to dramatically lower your heating and cooling costs.
