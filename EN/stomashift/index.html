Frequently Asked Questions

What is included in a whole home remodeling project?

A whole home remodeling project typically encompasses updates to multiple areas of the house simultaneously to ensure a cohesive design. This often includes kitchen and bathroom renovations, updating flooring and paint throughout, structural changes to improve flow, and upgrading electrical or plumbing systems as needed.

Why do I need a general contractor instead of hiring individual trades?

A general contractor acts as the project manager for your entire home renovation. They are responsible for coordinating all the different subcontractors (plumbers, electricians, carpenters), pulling necessary municipal permits, ensuring building codes are met, and keeping the project on schedule and budget. This saves you significant time and mitigates major risks.

What does "turnkey renovation" actually mean?

A turnkey renovation means the contractor handles absolutely every aspect of the project from the initial design and demolition to the final coat of paint and professional cleaning. When the project is completed, you literally just "turn the key" and move back into a fully finished, ready-to-use home without having to manage any lingering tasks.

How long does a home addition usually take?

The timeline for a home addition varies greatly depending on the scope, scale, and complexity of the build, as well as local permitting times. A simple room expansion might take a few months, whereas a multi-story addition could take six months to a year. Your general contractor will provide a detailed timeline during the planning phase.

Should I move out during a complete home renovation?

For a true whole home remodeling project that involves major structural changes, flooring replacement, and utility shut-offs, it is highly recommended and often necessary to relocate temporarily. Moving out allows the construction crew to work much faster and safer, ultimately reducing the total timeline of the project and keeping your family away from hazardous dust and noise.
